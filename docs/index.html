EYEreport

User Manual — clinical report composer for eye-care correspondence

EYEreport composes clinician-to-clinician letters — referral letters, examination reports, driver's-licence vision reports, and screening letters. You fill in a template, adjust a few fields, and generate a PDF stamped onto your office letterhead, ready to print, fax, or send to your EMR. Patient information stays on your device; the app sends nothing over the network.

Quick Start

Five minutes of one-time setup, then a report takes a couple of minutes.

  1. Set up your identity. Go to Settings and enter your name, credentials, and practitioner ID under PRACTITIONER. These print in the signature area of every report.
  2. Add your signature. Under SIGNATURE, tap Sign… and draw with your finger, Apple Pencil, or mouse — or Import from file… to use a scanned signature image (.png/.jpg).
  3. Import your letterhead. Under LETTERHEADS, tap Import letterhead PDF… and pick your office letterhead. Then tap the letterhead's name to open the calibration screen: drag the handles so the box outlines the blank writing area of your letterhead (avoiding the logo and footer), and drag the pin to where page numbers should print. Do this once per letterhead.
  4. Save your frequent referral recipients. Under PROVIDERS → Manage providers…, add the doctors you write to regularly (name and fax). You can also save them on the fly from inside a report.
  5. Write a report. Go to New Report, pick a template (or Blank Report), and fill in the fields — everything you type shows in red so your entries stand out from the boilerplate. Press Tab to jump field to field.
  6. Preview and send. Tap Preview. Check the letterhead and text size, then print it, save it as a PDF, or share it straight to your EMR — all from the preview toolbar.

How EYEreport Thinks

  • A report is a stack of blocks. Recipient, date, patient details, salutation, prose paragraphs, a findings grid, an Impressions/Plan box, closing, signature. Any letter type is just a different arrangement of these blocks.
  • Templates are starting points, not live links. Opening a template drops a copy into the editor; editing the copy never changes the template. Save any report back as a new template at any time — patient details are automatically stripped when you do.
  • Letterhead is separate from content. Any report can print on any letterhead (or plain paper). Your letterhead choice in Preview sticks until you change it.
  • One live draft. The report you're working on auto-saves continuously (encrypted) and survives quitting the app. There is exactly one draft — starting a new report replaces it, and Start Over discards it.

The New Report Tab

The chooser

  • Resume Draft appears at the top whenever a saved draft exists, showing the patient name and when it was last saved. Tap to continue exactly where you left off.
  • Blank Report starts from nothing; add blocks with the ＋ menu.
  • Templates lists your saved templates in your chosen order. Use the search field to filter by name.
  • Import report PDF… reopens a report you previously exported from EYEreport — see Reopening an Exported Report.
Starting a new report while a modified draft exists asks for confirmation first, so a half-finished report can't be lost by an accidental tap.

The editor

  • Fields you type into render in red on screen (never in the printed report) so data entry is visually distinct from fixed wording.
  • ＋ (Add Block) appends any block type, including a boxed prose block (framed text, like the Impressions/Plan box, for non-standard letters).
  • Arrange switches to arrange mode: drag blocks by their header to reorder, tap the trash to delete, and use the Add block here buttons to insert a block at any position. Deleting a block that contains text you typed asks for confirmation.
  • Start Over (top left) abandons the current report and clears the draft — with a warning if you've typed anything.
  • Save as Template / Preview sit side by side in the bottom bar. Save as Template stores the current structure and boilerplate (with patient details and dates blanked); Preview renders the finished PDF.

Block-by-Block Guide

Recipient

  • Provider + fax prints one line: To: Dr. Jane Smith  Fax: (403) 555-0100 ("Fax:" is omitted when empty). As you type the name, matching saved providers appear beneath the field — tap one, or press ↓/↑ then Return to fill name and fax without leaving the keyboard. A button below offers to add the current entry to your provider list (or update a saved fax).
  • Freeform is a single line for agencies ("To: Alberta Motor Vehicle Registry").
  • None prints nothing.

Date

Type day, month, year as numbers; the month shows as "AUG" when you leave the field, and prints that way ("9 AUG 2026"). A Today button appears beside the fields whenever the entered date isn't today — its presence is your stale-date flag; tap it to stamp the current date.

Patient

  • Name, date of birth, AHC, phone, and address, with a print-layout picker: Full box (bordered grid with labels) or Inline labels.
  • Each row's switch controls whether it prints — data stays put either way.
  • Edit fields unlocks structure: rename labels (e.g. AHC → "PHN"), reorder rows, delete rows, and restore deleted ones with Add field. Structure choices ride into templates.
  • Phone auto-formats to (999) 999-9999 and AHC to 99999-9999 when you leave the field.

Salutation

"Dear …," can mirror the recipient's name automatically (the default) — change the recipient and the salutation follows. Toggle mirroring off to type a different name; "To Whom It May Concern" and fully custom salutations are also available. The name prints exactly as entered, so include the title you want ("Dr. Smith", "Ms. Jones").

Prose

  • Free paragraphs with bold, italic, and underline from the formatting bar, plus bulleted and numbered lists.
  • Lists continue automatically: Return on a list item starts the next one; Return on an empty item ends the list.
  • Templates can contain pick-one choices (tap a segment or type your own wording) — those paragraphs show individual controls instead of the single text box.

Findings

  • An OD/OS grid. Values with built-in affordances — the "20/" prefix on acuity, "mmHg" after pressures — print automatically and are suppressed entirely when a value is left empty.
  • Each row's switch controls whether it prints; excluded rows dim but keep their data.
  • Edit fields unlocks the grid itself: rename rows, edit or clear the prefix/suffix, add, duplicate, delete, and reorder rows. A row can also span the full width instead of OD/OS.
  • Long grids paginate cleanly — column headers repeat on the next page, and a row never splits.

Impressions / Plan

Two fixed sections ("Impressions:", "Plan:"), each a rich-text box seeded with a ready-to-type bullet. One formatting bar drives both. The pair prints inside a single ruled box. Section headings are editable behind Edit fields.

Closing & Signature

The closing picker offers the standard sentences ("Thank you for sharing in the care…", "please contact our office…") or custom text. The signature block picks the valediction ("Regards," etc.) and shows a live preview of your signature ink and printed identity from the practitioner profile, with a shortcut to edit it in Settings. Printed reports leave wet-signing room when no signature image is on file.

Keyboard-First Editing

EYEreport is designed for an external keyboard:

  • Tab / Shift-Tab move through every field in the document, top to bottom, in visual order — including findings cells and each prose box.
  • Short fields select their contents when focused, so you can type straight over old values.
  • ↓/↑ + Return pick provider suggestions in the recipient block.
  • Dates are typed as numbers — no date-picker wheels.

Preview, Print & Export

  • Letterhead menu — pick any stored letterhead or "None (plain)". The choice is remembered app-wide until you change it (also settable by the radio buttons in Settings → LETTERHEADS).
  • Text size menu — 9–13 pt body text (default 11), re-rendered live. The choice is kept with the report and carried into templates saved from it.
  • Print — the system print dialog, fed the exact rendering on screen.
  • Save to Files — saves the PDF, named "LASTNAME, Firstname 2026-07-13.pdf", wherever you choose. The surname is capitalised the same way it prints on the letter, whether you typed the name or imported it.
  • Share — the share sheet, for AirDrop, Messages, or your EMR's share extension. The receiver gets a properly named PDF file.
The PDF is produced in Helvetica at true letter size, composited onto the letterhead within its calibrated safe zone. Multi-page reports stamp "Page N of M" at your calibrated position; single-page reports print no page number.

Importing Patient Details from IRIS

Rather than retyping a patient's details, EYEreport can read them straight out of an IRIS PDF — the file excerpt or the exam report. It picks up the name, date of birth, address and AHC number, plus the exam findings IRIS records: refraction and visual acuity (from the most recent subjective refraction), keratometry, and intraocular pressure.

Two ways in

  • Share it from IRIS (fewest steps): in the EMR's Share sheet, choose EYEreport. The app opens with the details waiting.
  • Import a saved file: Import patient details… on the New Report screen, or the Patient details button in the toolbar while a report is open.

What happens next

If a report is already open, a review sheet appears immediately. If you're still on the chooser, the details wait at the top of the screen under Patient Details Ready — pick your template as usual and the review sheet appears over the new report.

Nothing is filled in until you tap Apply to Report. The review sheet shows every value that was read, all of them editable, so you check them against the source first. Cancel applies nothing.

Where the exam values land. Findings go into your findings grid by row name — "IOP", "IOP (NCT)", "Tonometry", "Tonometry (NCT)" and "Intraocular pressure" all receive pressures; "K" or "Keratometry" receives keratometry; "Refraction" or "Rx" receives the refraction. If a value has no row to go to, the review sheet says so before you apply, and the rest still applies. The standard findings grid has no keratometry row — add one to your template if you want keratometry imported. Values are stored so they print correctly with your row's own "20/" prefix or "mmHg" suffix, and a row you've toggled off stays off.

Corrected and uncorrected acuity

Acuity measured with correction always comes across — it's recorded alongside the subjective refraction. Acuity measured without correction comes across when the exam recorded it under Entrance Skills as unaided.

Because IRIS records the viewing condition eye by eye, an eye tested with glasses has no unaided value to import — so a one-eyed result is normal, and only the eye that was tested unaided is filled in. Whatever is already in the other eye's cell is left alone.

Which row gets which is decided by the row's name:

  • A row marked sc — "VA (sc)", "VA unaided", "Uncorrected VA" — receives the uncorrected acuity, and only that.
  • A row marked cc — "VA (cc)", "BCVA" — receives the corrected acuity.
  • A row named just "VA" counts as corrected. Uncorrected acuity is never put into an unmarked row: if you want it, name the row so it says so.

Have both rows in a template and both get filled, in whichever order they sit in the grid. Have only a cc row on a day when uncorrected was measured, and the review sheet tells you the uncorrected value had nowhere to go rather than putting it somewhere wrong.

A combined "Refraction / VA" row

For a post-cataract follow-up you may want the refraction and the acuity it produced in one cell. Name a row so it mentions both — "Refraction / VA" — and it receives them together:

-1.00 -0.25 × 100°   20/20

Nothing else changes: a template with separate "Refraction" and "VA (cc)" rows still fills them separately, and a template with a combined row and separate rows fills all of them. If a refraction was recorded with no acuity, a combined row simply takes the refraction alone.

Check the name split. IRIS gives one name line, so EYEreport splits it into first and last — "Mary-Anne Van Der Berg" becomes Van Der Berg / Mary-Anne. A middle name stays with the first name. Where that guess can't be made reliably, correct it in the review sheet.
Privacy. A PDF shared into EYEreport is copied into the app by iOS; that copy is deleted the moment it's been read. Imported details are held in memory only and reach storage exactly where typed details do — inside the report, in the encrypted draft. Nothing is uploaded, and the details are not kept in any separate patient list.

Reopening an Exported Report

Every PDF EYEreport produces invisibly carries its own structured source. To reuse a past report — a follow-up letter a year later, or a correction — choose Import report PDF… on the New Report screen and pick the exported file. The report opens in the editor exactly as it was: patient details, findings values, impressions, formatting — everything editable again. Sharing one of your exported PDFs back into EYEreport from another app does the same thing — the app recognises its own reports and reopens them rather than treating them as a patient file to read.

Check the date. A reopened report keeps its original date — the Today button appears beside it as a reminder to restamp before resending. Only PDFs exported by EYEreport can be reopened this way; other PDFs (or files rewritten by third-party software) can't be reconstructed.

The Templates Tab

  • Reorder templates by long-press drag — the order here is the order on the New Report screen.
  • Rename with the pencil button.
  • Delete with the trash button.
  • The … menu exports all templates as a single file (share it to Files or a colleague) and imports a template file — imported templates are appended, never overwriting yours. Templates contain no patient data.

Backup & Restore

Export backup… writes one file (e.g. "EYEreport backup 2026-07-13") containing all templates, all letterheads with their calibrations, your practitioner profile including the signature, and your provider list. Import backup… restores it — after a clear confirmation, because importing replaces the app's current contents wholesale.

  • Keep a backup after any significant setup work, and before using Reset App to Defaults.
  • A backup contains no patient information — it is safe to store in Files or transfer to a new device.
  • Restoring on a fresh install brings back your entire working environment in one step.

Privacy Policy

Effective Date: July 13, 2026

EYEreport is designed to keep your information on your own device. The app does not require an account, does not communicate with developer-operated servers, and does not collect analytics, advertising identifiers, or other usage data.

Information Collection

The app does not collect, receive, or transmit personal information to the developer. All patient information, report content, and practitioner information remain on your device unless you explicitly choose to print, save, or share a document.

Patient Information

Patient names and clinical information may be entered while preparing reports. This information is used only to generate documents on your device. The developer never has access to this information.

The current working draft is stored locally on your device using encryption and is excluded from device backups. Starting a new report or choosing Start Over replaces the current draft.

Patient details may also be read from a PDF you import or share into the app from your EMR. When another app shares a PDF, iOS places a copy inside EYEreport; that copy is deleted as soon as it has been read. Details read from it are held in memory until you confirm them, and are then stored only as part of the report itself — the same place and the same encryption as details you type by hand. They are never uploaded, and the app keeps no separate patient list.

Stored App Data

Templates, practitioner information, saved providers, letterheads, and app settings are stored locally on your device. These items do not contain patient information and may be included in your normal device backups, depending on your backup settings.

Exported Documents

PDFs exported from EYEreport contain the patient information visible in the report. They also contain embedded document data that allows reports exported by EYEreport to be reopened for future editing using Import report PDF. Exported PDFs should therefore be handled as confidential clinical documents.

Data Transmission

EYEreport does not transmit information to the developer or to third-party servers. When you choose to print, save, AirDrop, email, fax, or otherwise share a document, that transfer is performed using services provided by your device or operating system.

Analytics and Tracking

EYEreport does not use analytics services, advertising frameworks, tracking technologies, crash-reporting services, or third-party data collection.

Security

Information remains under your control on your device. You are responsible for protecting your device, securing exported documents, and complying with applicable privacy legislation and professional standards in your jurisdiction.

Changes to This Policy

This Privacy Policy may be updated from time to time. Any revisions will be reflected by updating the Effective Date shown above.
